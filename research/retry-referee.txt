New alternative proof route identified by the independent referee
(complete mathematical argument for simplifying the residual cycle step;
not yet a Lean verification claim)

Shortestness/chordlessness of the dual cycle is stronger than necessary.
Take ANY simple cycle e_0,...,e_(k-1) in the simple cubic dual graph of a
linear2-regular3-uniform H. Let x_i be the original vertex representing its
cycle edge. Each x_i has exactly the two incident hyperedges e_i,e_(i+1),
so deleting all x_i kills exactly these k hyperedges. The x_i are distinct.

If k is even, alternating x_i hit all cycle hyperedges and the usual
4-per-chosen-vertex count closes the induction step without any condition
on chords or the third vertices.

If k is odd, an incidence parity argument guarantees a boundary vertex y:
there exists a vertex lying in exactly one hyperedge of F={e_i}. Otherwise
every vertex in the union of F would have both of its2 incidences in F,
so counting incidences would give3|F|=2|union F|, impossible for odd|F|.
Since every cycle-intersection x_i is incident with two edges of F, y differs
from every x_i. Its second incident edge lies outside F. Rotate the cycle
so that the sole edge of F containing y is its otherwise-uncovered edge.
Delete the k cycle-intersection vertices plus y. Exactly k+1 hyperedges
are killed; alternating(k-1)/2 cycle vertices plus y hit all of them.
Thus4*((k+1)/2)=(k+1)+(k+1), giving the required lift.

This argument works even if several third vertices along the cycle coincide
(chords in the dual graph). Only one boundary vertex is required in the odd
case. Accordingly the remaining infrastructure can be reduced to:
1. Construction of the simple cubic dual graph (nonlinear case already removed).
2. Existence of any simple cycle in a finite graph of minimum degree≥2.
3. Incidence-count parity boundary lemma for an odd finite hyperedge family.
4. Cyclic alternating selection and exact deletion accounting.
No shortest-cycle or induced-cycle theorem is intrinsically required.

Detailed matching audit:
1. A maximum-cardinality matching exists by maximizing cardinality over the
   finite powerset of the graph's edge set. Every edge is nonempty, so adding
   an edge disjoint from the matching support would increase its cardinality.
   Therefore every graph edge meets the matching support C.
2. For a matched edge{u,v}, suppose distinct unmatched vertices x,y satisfy
   ux,vy∈E. The edges ux and vy are disjoint because u,v lie in C whereas x,y
   do not, and x≠y. Neither is already matched. Removing uv and inserting
   ux,vy preserves all other matching disjointness and increases cardinality.
   The formal exchange proves exactly this no-augmenting-path-of-length3 fact.
3. A matched vertex has at most two unmatched neighbours: mapping x to its
   graph edge{u,x} injects those neighbours into incidentEdges(u)\{uv}, whose
   cardinality is degree(u)-1≤2. Simplicity is essential and correctly used.
4. If both ends u,v have unmatched neighbours, every such cross pair must be
   the same vertex, by step2. Hence each endpoint's unmatched-neighbour set
   has cardinality at most1, and their sum is at most2. If only one endpoint
   has such neighbours, step3 gives the same bound. The proof counts entries
   separately at the endpoints, so the common-neighbour triangle case is not
   mistakenly counted only once.
5. Every external edge (an edge not contained in C) meets C by maximality,
   and its other endpoint is outside C. Choose a matching edge containing its
   endpoint in C. It is counted in that matching edge's crossEdges family.
   Each such family has size≤2 by the endpoint count. The finite union bound
   therefore gives B:=number of external graph edges≤2k, k:=|M|. No unjustified
   unique-charge or exact union-cardinality assumption is used.
6. Matching edges are pairwise disjoint and each has2vertices, so |C|=2k.
   Let I=sum over graph edges e of |C∩e|. Incidence counting gives
   I≤sum over x∈C degree(x)≤3|C|=6k. Each internal edge contributes2 and each
   external edge contributes1, so I+B=2m. Thus2m≤8k, i.e.m≤4k.
