1. Theorem 4.6: 4 tau(H) <= |V(H)| + |E(H)| for finite 3-uniform H.

Primary sources successfully obtained:
A. V. Chvatal and C. McDiarmid, Small transversals in hypergraphs,
   Combinatorica 12 (1992), 19–26, DOI 10.1007/BF01191201.
   Correct author-upload URL:
   https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Vaclav-Chvatal/publication/226907175_Small_transversals_in_hypergraphs/links/5859098908ae3852d255658b/Small-transversals-in-hypergraphs.pdf
   Ordinary curl retrieved the 8-page PDF (370464 bytes), although the web
   search fetcher failed. Local research-only files:
   research/external/chvatal-mcdiarmid.pdf and .txt.
   Theorem 6.1 proof, Section 6, directly yields the k=3 result.
   Proof route: vertex-degree induction reduces to maximum degree two;
   pass to the dual multigraph on hyperedges; apply Shannon's edge-colouring
   theorem to find a matching, then complete a transversal.
   This introduces an unproved Shannon subcubic multigraph theorem unless
   it is separately formalized. No matching Lean result was located.

B. S. Thomasse and A. Yeo, Total domination of graphs and small transversals
   of hypergraphs, author manuscript at
   https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/stephan.thomasse/liste/strondom.pdf
   Full 10-page manuscript obtained. Local research-only copies:
   research/external/thomasse-yeo.pdf and .txt.
   Relevant result: Theorem 1, manuscript page 2 (Section 1).
   Its direct proof avoids Shannon. Induction handles high-degree vertices,
   edges containing both degree-one and degree-two vertices, and isolated
   edge components. The residual two-regular hypergraph contains a shortest
   loose cycle; parity determines a deletion and a set meeting deleted edges.
   The numerical decrease pays four per chosen vertex.

Remaining formal burden for route B, independently assessed:
- A finite deletion operation with exact edge/vertex bounds and preservation
  of 3-uniformity.
- Degree/incidence counting and the low-degree reduction cases.
- Existence of the required loose cycle in a finite two-regular hypergraph,
  including the two-edge/two-common-vertices case.
- Distinctness and external-incidence properties from shortestness.
- Even/odd index selection and the odd case's extra vertex/edge.
- Transversal extension and induction cardinal bookkeeping.
The existing finite hypergraph representation and alpha+tau bridge help,
but none of these missing assertions may be assumed. The source is available;
the obstacle is unfinished verified proof infrastructure, not a mathematical
open problem or lack of a proof in the literature.
